# Pipeline state — 13 Aug 2026

Where the gather → select → rerank path stands after a day of tracing it
layer by layer against real PDFs, what was changed, what the numbers say,
and what is still broken. Written as the working record for next week's
reranker evaluation.

Branch `recover-july-plus-quota-gather` (backend, 11 commits) and
`recover-july-reranker-ui` (frontend, 4 commits). Neither is pushed — the
SSH key on this machine authenticates as a GitHub account without write
access to `fairscape/*`.

---

## 1. The pipeline as it now runs

```
upload a PDF
  ├─ parse      title from page-1 layout, DOI from page 1 before the body
  ├─ enrich     OpenAlex, exact DOI lookup, falling back to title match
  └─ embed      SPECTER2 (cuda:0), title+abstract, body prefix if no abstract

build a profile
  ├─ seeds      profile_seeds
  ├─ topics     OpenAlex topics from the seeds
  │             + up to 10 mapped from UMLS concepts, computed on demand
  └─ calibrate  dry-run: gather + select, NO rerank, NO persist

commit, then gather
  ├─ gather     one OpenAlex query per enabled topic, equal quota each
  ├─ select     SPECTER2 cosine to the seed centroid, threshold applied HERE
  ├─ rerank     MedCPT cross-encoder, blended 40:60 with the selector
  └─ persist    profile_candidates

daily feed      ordered by COALESCE(score_blended, score)
```

Two things about this shape are worth holding onto:

**The threshold runs before the reranker.** `selector.select(candidates,
profile, threshold=profile.threshold)`. Papers the selector rates below
θ never reach the cross-encoder, so it cannot promote them. This turned
out to matter far more than expected — see §4.

**The dry-run does not rerank.** What the wizard shows during
calibration is pure selector output; what the daily feed serves is
reranked. The two orderings differ. July's plan had wiring the reranker
into the dry-run as step 12; it was never done.

## 3. What was changed, and the evidence for each

### Parsing — title and DOI were wrong on every paper tried

Title came back as a running head, a masthead or a submission date stamp
three times out of three on the first sample set. Now taken from the
largest type on page 1, with four corrections each forced by a layout
that beat the previous attempt: modal character size per row (a section
drop cap otherwise outranks the title), word gaps rebuilt from
x-coordinates (PDFs store spaces as displacement), rows split at column
gutters (a margin-set journal name otherwise merges into the title), and
only the upper half of the page considered (JMIR stamps a 20pt `XSLFO`
in the footer). The embedded metadata title is reconciled against the
rendered one rather than preferred outright.

DOI: the regex began with `\b`, and extraction routinely drops the space
before the identifier — `DigitalObjectIdentifier10.1109/...` has no word
boundary between `r` and `1`. The paper's own DOI was skipped and the
first cleanly spaced one taken instead, which lives in the reference
list. **A wrong DOI is worse than none**: enrichment looks it up exactly,
so the seed silently acquires another paper's title, abstract and
topics. This is the mechanism behind July's "obesity" failure, where an
obesity paper carried a gravitational-lens title — recorded at the time
as an OpenAlex data problem, which it was not.

Verified: 9 of 9 PDFs across four sets now resolve by exact DOI lookup
with matching titles.

### Embedding — a paper with no abstract went in on 20 words

The drop order (body, substances, keywords, mesh) is right while the
abstract carries the paper. Without one it discards the only substantial
text there is. One of nine uploads had no OpenAlex abstract and entered
the centroid on 20 words against 161–464 for its neighbours. The body
was in `body_text` the whole time, and since the PDF does print an
abstract, a prefix of it recovers what OpenAlex was missing. 20 → 512
words, full structured abstract inside the window.

### UMLS — three mechanisms were dropping the topics it existed to add

On the type-2 profile, OpenAlex tagged both seeds only as "Artificial
Intelligence in Healthcare" and "Machine Learning in Healthcare" — no
diabetes topic at all. UMLS extracted the diabetes concepts and mapped
them to real OpenAlex diabetes topics. None reached the profile:

1. `Research Activity`, `Mental Process` and `Natural Phenomenon` were
   admitted as relevant semantic types. Every paper is a Research
   Activity, so the type says nothing about a paper's subject — but its
   concepts ("Clinical Research", "research study") map onto OpenAlex's
   own generic topic names ("Various Academic Research Studies") at a
   *higher* cosine than a specific concept matches a specific topic.
   They took six of ten places.
2. The cap was 4.
3. Deduplication between candidates used an embedding threshold
   calibrated on synonyms (0.806–0.854) versus unrelated pairs
   (0.512–0.687). Sibling topics inside one field were not in that set
   and measure 0.719–0.815, straddling it. Accepting "Diabetes and
   associated disorders" discarded "Diabetes, Cardiovascular Risks"
   (0.761) and "Diabetes Treatment and Management" (0.815) as duplicates
   of it.

Diabetes topics in the merged list went 0 → 1 → 2 → 4 as each was fixed.

Extraction input also now leads with the title. Abstracts introduce an
abbreviation once and use it throughout, and the linker only expands one
whose expansion is in the same text; a paper whose abstract said "T2DM"
nine times yielded no diabetes concept until the title was prepended.

### Gather — the quota capped the total, and copies took slots

`per_topic_quota` is what a topic is guaranteed when several compete; it
must not also decide the total. A two-topic profile capped at 20 papers
however much OpenAlex had. The dry-run suffered worst — it asks for
`DRY_RUN_FETCH_LIMIT` over 30 days precisely so the threshold sweep has
a distribution, and got 50 where the material supported 896. The share
is now derived from what the caller asked for, with the constant as a
floor.

Deduplication was by `openalex_id` alone. OpenAlex carries the preprint,
the version of record and the conference copy as separate works; the
same paper took two quota slots and appeared twice in the top ten on
every trial run. Caught during the fetch now, which frees the slot,
keeps `n_fetched` honest, and makes the dry-run agree with the gather it
previews.

### Reranker — the weights meant nothing like what they said

`alpha=0.4 / beta=0.6` was not a 40:60 split. The reranker's min-max
fills [0,1] by construction; the selector was mapped through a fixed
`(cos+1)/2`, and real cosines within a batch span a band a twentieth as
wide. What moves a ranking is weight times spread: 0.4 × 0.057 = 0.023
against 0.6 × 1.0 = 0.600, an **effective 4:96**, with the blend
correlating 0.997 with the reranker alone and 0.014 with the selector.
Both stages are now min-maxed over the same batch. Measured after:
40.0 : 60.0. The shipped values became correct; before the fix, reaching
a real 40:60 would have needed alpha near 0.92.

`query_mode` mattered more than the weights. On one profile, same
candidates throughout:

| mode | selector↔reranker rank correlation |
| --- | --- |
| `article` (seed title+abstract, ~300 words) | **−0.04** |
| `topic`, polluted topic list | +0.15 |
| `topic`, clean topic list | **+0.62** |

`article` puts a document where this model's query side saw PubMed
search strings. Under it an industrial paper-drying study went from #24
to #1 and six genuine diabetes papers were pushed past #40. `.env` is
back to `topic`. A `title` mode was added — seed titles name disease,
measurement and method in one string, which topic names cannot — but it
is not the default; on the one profile measured it moved a single paper.

### Reporting — two display defects, one of them load-bearing

`topic_filters_to_topics` hardcoded `on=True` and dropped `source`, so
the profile page showed every topic as gathering and every topic as
OpenAlex's. A profile with ten of fourteen switched off displayed "14
TOPICS", unlabelled, while the gather correctly used four. A second
mapper in `routers/profiles.py` reads both fields correctly — that
divergence is why it survived, since the wizard's step 3 looked right.

Cards were bucketed on `score`. `BUCKET_HIGH` and `BUCKET_MEDIUM` are
0.95 and 0.925 — top 5% and top 7.5% — which only reads against a rank
percentile. Applied to a similarity whose range depends on the
normalisation (0.008–0.909 for a blend of two min-maxed stages),
essentially nothing qualified: **874 of 875 live cards were "low"**, and
the top-ranked paper displayed "100%" in grey. Now bucketed on
`score_pct`, the value the card already prints.

### Upload — a background thread stalled the next request

Extraction ran on a daemon thread so the upload could return early. It
holds the GIL through spaCy and the ollama calls; request bodies are
read on the event loop; the next upload stalled mid-stream. One ran 312
seconds and failed as "uploaded file is empty". Read endpoints went from
~100 ms to 2.4–3.3 s in that window. Retrying once idle: 5.5 s. The
thread is gone; `_try_merge_umls_topics` computes what a profile's seeds
are missing at step 3, which is the only place the result is read.

## 4. What three live profiles show

All gathered through the UI, one day of OpenAlex, threshold 0.85.

| profile | topics | pool on-subject | top ~12 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| type-2 diabetes | 4, no diabetes topic among them | 12/179 = **7%** | 3 of 12 |
| type-1 diabetes | 15, 10 of them UMLS noise | 83/299 = **28%** | 11 of 12 |
| infectious disease | 18, 3 of them noise | 154/397 = **39%** | **14 of 14** |

The ordering is entirely explained by the topic set, and nothing
downstream compensates for it. The type-2 profile is the July slide-5
failure reproduced exactly: query "AI in Healthcare" and "ML in
Healthcare" and OpenAlex returns generic medical AI, of which 7% happens
to concern diabetes. The selector put the best two of those at #1 and #2
with cosines of 0.974 — **it did its job**; it cannot return what the
pool does not contain. Fifth place was a healthcare insurance fraud
detection paper.

**UMLS is not uniformly good or bad — it depends on whether OpenAlex got
the seeds right.** For type-2 it supplied the only diabetes topics that
profile has. For type-1 it added food composition, plant reproductive
biology, lipid metabolism and colorectal cancer screening, which took
54% of the quota and 10 of 15 API calls. The right unit of decision is
the individual topic, not the source.

The infectious-disease profile is the one case where a topic expressing
the actual conjunction appeared — `T10506 Aquaculture disease management
and microbiota` — and it delivered: 11 of 397 candidates are both
infectious-disease and marine, the only non-zero intersection of the
three.

### On the reranker specifically

Across roughly 64 configurations measured on one profile (four query
modes × two aggregations × weights, with and without the noise topics),
**no configuration beat the selector alone** on a keyword proxy; the best
tied it. Degradation was monotonic in beta in every row group, and `mean`
aggregation beat `max` everywhere.

That was measured with `threshold=None`, which is **not** what production
does. In the live gather the threshold filters before the reranker sees
anything, and the damage is correspondingly smaller: on the type-1 feed
one food-science paper reached #3, against the wholesale disruption the
probe showed. The probe overstated the harm.

What remains true is that no measurement so far demonstrates a *benefit*.
The honest position is "configuration repaired, value unproven".

## 6. Next

1. **Do not tune the reranker further before there are labels.** The
   parameter space is mapped; what is missing is a relevance judgement.
   50–100 hand-labelled candidates across the profiles, then nDCG@10 or
   P@10 comparing selector-alone against the blend. The keyword proxy
   used so far cannot resolve a one-paper difference, and it
   under-counts: it missed "SARS-CoV-2" and "Japanese Encephalitis" as
   infectious-disease titles.
2. Start that evaluation from `topic` mode, `mean` aggregation, low
   beta, and a curated topic list. `article` and `max` are excluded by
   the measurements above.
3. The selector has never been A/B'd. `centroid.py` already computes
   `score_max_seed` and `score_median_seed` for every candidate and
   ranks by neither; `MaxSeedSelector` is implemented and registered.
   Comparing the three orderings costs nothing — the numbers are already
   in the breakdown.
4. Neither branch is pushed.
